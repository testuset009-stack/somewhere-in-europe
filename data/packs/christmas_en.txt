#1 [UA]
In this country, the centerpiece of Christmas Eve supper is sweet wheat porridge called kutia, often accompanied by a ceremonial wheat sheaf called a didukh.
+++
Tradition here calls for 12 meatless dishes on Christmas Eve, and children carry festive supper to their godparents.
---
Ukraine — Kyiv. The didukh honors ancestors and a good harvest, bringing generations together at one table.

#2 [FI]
Lapland in this Nordic country is home to the official Santa Claus Village, where hundreds of thousands of letters arrive every winter.
+++
On Christmas Eve, families traditionally visit a hot sauna together before sitting down for the festive dinner.
---
Finland — Helsinki. Rovaniemi sits right on the Arctic Circle and welcomes visitors to Joulupukki's workshop.

#3 [DE]
This country is famous for inventing the indoor Christmas tree tradition and the classic open-air Advent markets known as Weihnachtsmärkte.
+++
Paper advent calendars with 24 little doors to count down to Christmas Eve also began here.
---
Germany — Berlin. Records of decorated fir trees and winter town markets date back here to the 16th century.

#4 [AT]
In this Alpine nation, St. Nicholas is accompanied in early December by Krampus, a horned, furry folklore creature who rattles chains.
+++
In 1818, the world-famous carol "Silent Night" was performed for the very first time in the village of Oberndorf here.
---
Austria — Vienna. Franz Xaver Gruber and Joseph Mohr wrote "Stille Nacht" just before midnight mass.

#5 [PL]
On Christmas Eve here, families tuck a handful of hay beneath a white tablecloth and always leave one empty chair for an unexpected guest.
---
Poland — Warsaw. Before eating, everyone breaks a thin Christmas wafer called opłatek and exchanges warm wishes.

#6 [IT]
Children here receive gifts not only at Christmas, but also on January 6 from La Befana, a kind old witch who flies on a broomstick.
+++
No festive table is complete without panettone, a tall dome-shaped sweet bread studded with raisins and candied fruit.
---
Italy — Rome. La Befana slides down chimneys at Epiphany to fill stockings with sweets for good kids.

#7 [ES]
In Catalonia in this country, children care for and cover a smiling wooden log called Tió de Nadal so it will magically "drop" treats on Christmas Eve.
---
Spain — Madrid. Across most of the country, major presents arrive later on January 6 from the Three Wise Men.

#8 [IS]
Instead of one Santa, 13 mischievous Yule Lads visit over 13 nights, and the favorite Christmas Eve gift is a brand-new book.
+++
This cozy custom is called Jólabókaflóð — the "Christmas Book Flood" — when everyone reads all night with hot chocolate.
---
Iceland — Reykjavik. More books are bought per person here in December than almost anywhere else on Earth.

#9 [SE]
In the town of Gävle in this Scandinavian country, residents build a giant 13-meter straw goat every December.
---
Sweden — Stockholm. December 13 also brings St. Lucia's Day, celebrated with glowing candle crowns and saffron buns.

#10 [NO]
According to an old folk belief in this country, families hide every broom and mop on Christmas Eve so mischievous spirits cannot fly away on them.
---
Norway — Oslo. Families also eat rice porridge called riskrem with one hidden almond inside for good luck.

#11 [DK]
Christmas dinner here ends with a cold rice pudding topped with warm cherry sauce, and whoever finds the single whole almond wins a marzipan pig.
+++
Woven paper Christmas hearts (julehjerter) hung on trees also originated in this country.
---
Denmark — Copenhagen. The dessert is called risalamande, and everyone hides the almond in their mouth to keep the suspense going!

#12 [GB]
At Christmas dinner here, guests cross arms and pull cardboard "crackers" that pop open to reveal a paper crown, a joke, and a tiny toy.
+++
The meal finishes with a rich steamed pudding set aflame and eaten around the monarch's holiday broadcast.
---
United Kingdom — London. Everyone at the table wears the colorful tissue-paper crown throughout the meal.

#13 [FR]
The signature holiday dessert in this country is a chocolate-and-cream sponge cake shaped and frosted like a wooden Yule log — Bûche de Noël.
---
France — Paris. The pastry log recalls an ancient custom of burning a blessed log in the hearth on Christmas Eve.

#14 [CZ]
Children here are told that if they fast until the first evening star appears on Christmas Eve, they will spot a lucky "golden piglet" on the wall.
+++
Families also slice an apple crosswise: if the seeds form a neat star, the coming year brings health and joy.
---
Czechia — Prague. Fried carp with potato salad remains the classic holiday dinner across the country.

#15 [GR]
Along the coasts and islands of this sunny country, people traditionally decorate wooden model sailboats with fairy lights — called karavaki — alongside or instead of trees.
---
Greece — Athens. The illuminated boats honor sailors and symbolize safe voyages into the new year.

#16 [NL]
The main gift-giving night in this country happens on December 5, when Sinterklaas arrives by steamboat with his white horse.
---
Netherlands — Amsterdam. Children leave shoes stuffed with carrots and hay by the door for Sinterklaas's horse.

#17 [EE]
The medieval Town Hall Square of this Baltic capital hosted one of the world's very first public Christmas trees back in 1441.
---
Estonia — Tallinn. Tallinn's cobblestone square still holds one of Northern Europe's coziest winter markets.

#18 [LV]
Set in the square of this country's capital is a plaque commemorating a decorated spruce put up in 1510, adorned with paper roses.
---
Latvia — Riga. Winter traditions here also include cheerful mummers dressed as bears, cranes, and wolves bringing good fortune.

#19 [LT]
Christmas Eve here is called Kūčios and features 12 meatless dishes, including tiny baked biscuits called kūčiukai served in sweet poppy-seed milk.
---
Lithuania — Vilnius. Families often leave a few dishes untouched overnight out of respect for departed ancestors.

#20 [PT]
The traditional Christmas Eve meal called Consoada here centers on boiled salted cod (bacalhau) with potatoes and greens, followed by honey-cinnamon treats.
---
Portugal — Lisbon. On Christmas morning, families share a ring-shaped Bolo Rei ("King Cake") studded with jewel-like candied fruits.

#21 [IE]
On Christmas Eve in this island nation, families light a tall candle in the front window as a beacon of warmth and welcome to any traveler.
---
Ireland — Dublin. Many brave locals also take a freezing Christmas Day charity dip in the sea!

#22 [RO]
During the holidays here, children walk from house to house carrying a decorated cardboard star on a pole — called Steaua — while singing carols.
---
Romania — Bucharest. Festive tables feature cabbage rolls (sarmale) and sweet braided cozonac bread filled with walnuts and cocoa.

#23 [HU]
Holiday tables here always feature swirled poppy-seed and walnut rolls called bejgli, while trees are draped in foil-wrapped fondant candies called szaloncukor.
---
Hungary — Budapest. Kids love sneaking the candies out of the foil wrappers while leaving the shiny paper hanging on the branches!

#24 [CH]
In the German-speaking cantons of this mountainous country, Samichlaus visits children with nuts, mandarins, and gingerbread, joined by helper Schmutzli.
---
Switzerland — Bern. Towns also hold "Klausjagen" parades where participants carry towering glowing paper lanterns.

#25 [BE]
On December 6 here, children celebrate Saint Nicholas with figurines made of world-famous local chocolate and spiced speculoos cookies.
---
Belgium — Brussels. Bakeries fill the winter streets with the warm aroma of cinnamon, nutmeg, and caramelized speculoos.

#26 [SK]
Before Christmas Eve dinner in this country, the head of the house tosses a spoonful of sweet poppy-seed porridge called lokša toward the ceiling — the more sticks, the richer the harvest!
+++
Dinner often starts with a thin wafer smeared with honey and a clove of garlic for good health.
---
Slovakia — Bratislava. After cabbage-and-mushroom soup (kapustnica), children open gifts beneath the tree.

#27 [HR]
Along this sunny Adriatic country, families sow wheat seeds into a bowl on December 13 so green sprouts decorate the tree by Christmas Eve.
+++
Its capital's winter Advent market has repeatedly been voted the best Christmas market in Europe.
---
Croatia — Zagreb. A tricolor ribbon is often tied around the lush green Christmas wheat bowl.

#28 [SI]
In this small Alpine country, St. Nicholas processions called Miklavževanje walk the streets in early December, while tables feature rolled walnut bread called potica.
---
Slovenia — Ljubljana. Every winter, thousands visit the famous living Nativity staged inside the Postojna Cave.

#29 [BG]
On Christmas Eve here, families prepare an odd number of meatless dishes and bake a round pita bread hiding a lucky coin inside.
+++
On January 1, children tap neighbors on the back with a decorated cornel-wood twig called a survachka for health.
---
Bulgaria — Sofia. Whoever finds the hidden coin in their slice of pita bread is promised a prosperous year.

#30 [RS]
On Christmas Eve here, families bring home an oak branch with dry leaves called a badnjak and burn it for warmth and good fortune.
+++
A coin is also baked into the round festive bread called česnica, broken by hand at the table.
---
Serbia — Belgrade. Christmas is celebrated on January 7, starting with a lucky first visitor known as the polaznik.

#31 [MD]
Winter carolers in this country carry embroidered bags, ring bells, and walk with a decorated wooden plow called Pluguşorul wishing for an abundant harvest.
---
Moldova — Chisinau. Holiday tables feature cabbage rolls (sarmale), flaky plăcinte pies, and sweet braided crăciun bread.

#32 [BY]
Winter Kaliady celebrations in this country feature mummers carrying a spinning star and a "goat" mask, with three ceremonial types of kutia porridge cooked across the season.
---
Belarus — Minsk. Its ancient Belovezhskaya Pushcha forest hosts a snowy winter residence beneath towering firs.

#33 [LU]
In this small grand duchy tucked between Belgium, France, and Germany, Kleeschen delivers treats on December 6 into shoes left outside bedroom doors.
---
Luxembourg — Luxembourg. Winter street markets here serve crispy potato pancakes called Gromperekichelcher.

#34 [MK]
On January 5 in this Balkan country, children walk neighborhood streets singing "Kolede" songs to collect walnuts, chestnuts, and coins around evening bonfires.
---
North Macedonia — Skopje. The next evening, families break a flat bread containing a lucky coin.

#35 [ME]
In this mountainous Adriatic nation, communities gather before churches on Badnji Dan to bless and light towering oak badnjak logs, while straw is scattered on floors at home.
---
Montenegro — Podgorica. Folk tradition says every rising spark from the oak log brings good luck in the new year.

#36 [AL]
In late December here, families decorate a sparkling "New Year tree", bake walnut-and-honey baklava, and exchange gifts beneath winter fireworks.
---
Albania — Tirana. Skanderbeg Square in the capital turns into a bustling winter village with carousels and lights.

#37 [BA]
Winter festivities in this country's capital celebrate its 1984 Winter Olympic heritage alongside strong Bosnian coffee, walnut baklava, and traditional holiday feasts.
---
Bosnia and Herzegovina — Sarajevo. Snowy peaks like Jahorina and Bjelašnica right above the capital fill with skiers every December.

#38 [UA]
The melody "Shchedryk" by composer Mykola Leontovych from this country became one of the world's best-known Christmas songs under the title "Carol of the Bells".
---
Ukraine — Kyiv. "Shchedryk" was first performed in Kyiv in 1916 by the Kyiv University choir.

#39 [DE]
The city of Nuremberg in this country has baked famous spiced honey gingerbread called Lebkuchen on thin wafers for over four centuries.
---
Germany — Berlin. Most German Christmas markets serve warm spiced drinks in collectible dated ceramic mugs.

#40 [IT]
A single street in Naples in this country — Via San Gregorio Armeno — is lined with artisan workshops crafting handmade Nativity figurines (presepe) all year round.
---
Italy — Rome. Alongside traditional shepherds, artisans often carve humorous miniature bakers, musicians, and even footballers!
